Good questions before gutter work.

How should I describe a gutter problem when requesting service?

Give the location, what water or debris is doing, and when you notice it. A seam leak, an edge overflow, and a blocked outlet may need different evaluations. A safe ground-level photo can help identify the area, but do not climb to inspect or demonstrate the condition.

What determines the gutter size for a roof section?

The area and pitch of the roof, valley flow, run length, outlet arrangement, downspouts, and discharge route are considered together. The estimate should explain which runs receive a selected profile; size alone cannot correct a downstream restriction.

Can I rely on gutter guards to prevent future blockages?

No. A guard may reduce some debris entry, but material can collect on its surface or reach the channel, and outlets still need attention. Ask how the selected product is inspected and maintained before comparing options.

Does a gutter estimate include roof or underground drainage work?

Not unless those items are written into the scope. Roof defects, fascia reconstruction, grading, and buried piping are distinct from ordinary gutter service. Confirm what is included and who evaluates any related condition.
